# Grounding Exercises for Anxiety and Distress

## What is Grounding?

Grounding techniques help bring you back to the present moment when you're feeling overwhelmed, anxious, or disconnected. They work by redirecting your focus from distressing thoughts to your immediate environment.

## Physical Grounding

### The 5-4-3-2-1 Technique
This is one of the most effective grounding exercises:

1. **5 THINGS YOU CAN SEE**
   Look around and name five things you can see right now.
   Example: "I see a blue lamp, a window, my phone, a plant, a book"

2. **4 THINGS YOU CAN TOUCH**
   Notice the texture and temperature of four things.
   Example: "I feel the soft fabric of my shirt, the cool desk, rough carpet, smooth phone"

3. **3 THINGS YOU CAN HEAR**
   Listen for three distinct sounds.
   Example: "I hear traffic outside, the AC humming, birds chirping"

4. **2 THINGS YOU CAN SMELL**
   Notice two scents (or imagine calming scents).
   Example: "I smell coffee, fresh air"

5. **1 THING YOU CAN TASTE**
   Notice any taste in your mouth, or take a sip of water.
   Example: "I taste mint from my toothpaste"

### Body Awareness Exercise
1. Press your feet firmly into the floor
2. Feel the weight of your body in the chair
3. Squeeze your hands together, then release
4. Roll your shoulders back and down
5. Take three slow breaths

### Temperature Grounding
- Hold an ice cube in your hand
- Splash cold water on your face
- Hold a warm cup of tea or coffee
- Take a cold or warm shower

## Mental Grounding

### Categories Game
Pick a category and name as many items as you can:
- Countries that start with "A"
- Types of animals
- Songs by your favorite artist
- Colors you can see in the room

### Math Grounding
- Count backwards from 100 by 7s
- Add up the digits in your phone number
- Multiply simple numbers in your head

### Memory Lane
- Describe your bedroom in detail
- Recall the plot of your favorite movie
- Think through the steps of a recipe you know

## Soothing Grounding

### Safe Place Visualization
1. Close your eyes (or soften your gaze)
2. Imagine a place where you feel completely safe
3. Notice the colors, sounds, smells, temperature
4. Let yourself feel the safety of this place
5. Stay as long as you need

### Self-Soothing Statements
Repeat to yourself:
- "I am safe right now"
- "This feeling will pass"
- "I can handle this"
- "I am in the present moment"

## Quick Grounding (30 seconds or less)

When you need instant grounding:

1. **Splash Protocol**: Cold water on face activates the dive reflex
2. **Press and Release**: Squeeze fists tight for 5 seconds, then release
3. **Name Game**: Say your name, today's date, and where you are
4. **Breath Focus**: Take one slow breath, noticing it completely

## When to Use Grounding

Grounding is helpful when you're experiencing:
- Anxiety or panic
- Flashbacks or intrusive memories
- Feeling disconnected or "spacey"
- Overwhelming emotions
- Difficulty concentrating

## Practice Tips

- Practice when you're calm so it's easier when stressed
- Find 2-3 techniques that work best for you
- Keep a list of your go-to exercises on your phone
- Remember: grounding takes practice

## Grounding vs. Distraction

Grounding is not about escaping your feelings—it's about:
- Returning to the present moment
- Reducing the intensity of overwhelming emotions
- Creating space to think clearly
- Reconnecting with your body and surroundings
